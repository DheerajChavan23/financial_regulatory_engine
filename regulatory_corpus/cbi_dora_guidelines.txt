REGULATORY OPERATIONAL RESILIENCE AND CLAIMS INGESTION GUIDELINES
Document Reference: CBI-DORA-REG-2026-V1
Effective Date: 17 January 2025 (Statutory DORA Enforcement)
Jurisdiction: Ireland / European Union
Supervisory Authority: Central Bank of Ireland (Banc Ceannais na hÉireann)

SECTION 1: PURPOSE AND SCOPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 These Guidelines establish the statutory compliance framework for regulated financial
entities, insurance undertakings (Designated Activity Companies), and ICT third-party service
providers operating within the State under Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (DORA) and the Central
Bank of Ireland Cross-Industry Guidance on Operational Resilience.

1.2 All digital claims ingestion engines, automated document processors, and operational
audit pipelines must rigorously apply the mandatory standards herein regarding incident
classification, statutory notification deadlines, policy identification, financial integrity,
and data governance.


SECTION 2: DORA INCIDENT CLASSIFICATION TIERS (ARTICLE 18)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Financial entities must assess and classify operational and technological incidents
into one of three defined regulatory tiers:
    (a) Major ICT-Related Incident (DORA Article 18): An incident having a potential or
        actual high adverse impact on the network and information systems supporting critical
        or important functions of the financial entity. Quantitative criteria include:
        - Financial impact exceeding €100,000 (direct losses or remediation);
        - Disruption lasting greater than 2 hours to client-facing services;
        - Data integrity compromise or unauthorized cyber intrusion.
    (b) Significant Operational Disruption: Incidents causing material service degradation,
        fleet logistical breakdown, or property damage without compromising core banking/ICT
        backbones.
    (c) Standard Operational Claim (Non-ICT): Routine property, casualty, or commercial
        indemnity claims arising from localized non-technological events.

2.2 The regulatory classification tier assigned at inbound ingestion must be preserved
across all data lakehouse layers (Bronze, Silver, Gold) for supervisory reporting.


SECTION 3: STATUTORY NOTIFICATION TIMELINES AND SLA LIMITS (ARTICLE 19)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Major ICT Incidents:
    - Initial Notification: Must be submitted to the Central Bank of Ireland within 4 hours
      from incident classification, and no later than 24 hours from becoming aware of the incident.
    - Intermediate Report: Required within 72 hours detailing mitigation measures.
    - Final Report: Mandatory within 1 month following complete incident remediation.

3.2 Operational Claims Submission Deadline SLA:
    - Under CBI Insurance Claims Protocols, formal insurance and loss dossiers must be
      lodged with the insurer within a maximum of 90 calendar days from the date of the incident.
    - Claims submitted with a notification latency exceeding 90 days represent statutory SLA
      breaches and potential statute of limitations violations.
    - Ingestion engines must automatically flag and quarantine any claim record where:
          (Filing_Date - Incident_Date) > 90 days.
    - Quarantined records may not be promoted to Gold reporting until certified by an authorized
      claims examiner or legal compliance officer.


SECTION 4: MANDATORY POLICY IDENTIFICATION AND CONTRACTUAL VALIDITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Contractual Binding Requirement:
    - Every claim dossier submitted for automated ingestion and financial liquidation must
      bear a valid, authenticated Policy Number (e.g., POL-IE-XXXXXX).
    - Under the Central Bank of Ireland Consumer Protection Code, financial liquidation
      cannot be initiated against unverified or missing contracts.

4.2 Missing Policy Protocol:
    - Submissions where the Policy Number is omitted, null, or populated with placeholder
      tokens (e.g., 'N/A', 'None', '[MISSING]') must fail schema validation immediately.
    - The ingestion engine must divert such dossiers to Quarantine with the audit failure tag:
      'RULE_POLICY_NUMBER_PRESENT'.


SECTION 5: FINANCIAL INTEGRITY AND VALUATION RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 Positive Financial Valuation Standard:
    - Claims submitted for indemnity liquidation must represent genuine, verifiable, positive
      financial expenditures incurred during incident remediation or property restoration.
    - Aggregate Gross Loss, Total Deductible, and Net Claim Liquidation amounts must be strictly
      greater than zero (Total_Net_Claimed > 0.00 EUR).

5.2 Negative Liquidation Prohibition:
    - Any claim displaying a negative net liquidation request (Net Claim < €0.00) constitutes
      a critical financial irregularity, anomalous chargeback error, or potential fraud indicator.
    - Ingestion pipelines must reject and quarantine all negative amount records under:
      'RULE_POSITIVE_AMOUNTS'.

5.3 Ledger Reconciliation:
    - The cumulative sum of individual itemized loss line items (net of deductible) must
      reconcile with the aggregate requested net claim within a statutory rounding tolerance
      of ±€0.05. Discrepancies exceeding this threshold require automated quarantine.


SECTION 6: DATA GOVERNANCE, GDPR, AND IRISH PPSN PROTECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 Protection of Restricted Identifiers:
    - Under the Irish Data Protection Act 2018 and GDPR Article 4(5), Irish Personal Public
      Service Numbers (PPSNs) represent restricted national identification numbers.
    - Plaintext PPSNs must NEVER be stored, persisted, or exported to downstream analytical
      or reporting lakehouse tiers (Silver and Gold).

6.2 Cryptographic Pseudonymization Mandate:
    - PPSNs must be transformed at the Bronze-to-Silver boundary using a cryptographically
      salted, keyed HMAC-SHA256 one-way hashing function.
    - The resulting pseudonym (prefixed with 'PPSN_') provides irreversible unlinkability to
      external parties while preserving deterministic joins for internal statutory audit.

6.3 Contact and Geographic Masking:
    - Personal names, contact telephone numbers, and email addresses must be masked.
    - Street-level addresses must be generalized to County and Eircode Routing Key (first 3
      characters) to prevent individual re-identification while preserving regional actuarial
      risk modeling capabilities.


SECTION 7: IMMUTABLE AUDITABILITY AND PROVENANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1 Bronze Ingestion Immutability:
    - Every raw inbound file must be preserved in its pristine, untransformed state with a
      cryptographic SHA-256 digest and UUID ingestion identifier.
    - Lakehouse promotion timestamps must be recorded in UTC ISO 8601 format.

7.2 Supervisory Inspection Access:
    - Regulated entities must ensure that automated compliance audit engines can cross-reference
      any ingested claim against these regulatory guidelines using Retrieval-Augmented Generation
      (RAG) and semantic vector search, generating auditable compliance citations on demand.
